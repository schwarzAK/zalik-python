Last semester I decided to study in the morning rather than at night. The first week was hard: the alarm rang at six, and it seemed to me that I would never get anything done. But by the second week a rhythm appeared. I left the dormitory at seven, bought coffee at a kiosk near the metro and rode to the university while the streets were still empty. It was quiet in the library at that hour, and in two hours I managed to do as much as I used to do in a whole evening. At first my classmates laughed at me, and then two of them joined in. Now we go through lectures together, explain difficult topics to each other and prepare for exams without any last-minute night panic. The main lesson for me is simple: discipline matters more than inspiration, and a small, well-planned effort every day is better than a heroic sprint before the exam session.
